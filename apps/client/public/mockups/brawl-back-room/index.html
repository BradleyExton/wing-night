Streets of Barrie — the back room

A sketch for docs/minigames/ideas/brawl-back-room-finale.md: the finale block laid out end to end, to answer one question — what is "inside a building" on a street that only scrolls one way? Nothing here is final art, and nothing is built. It is drawn in the sim's own units: four windows of 192 × 108, the walkable band 32 deep from y 70.

The rooms are the windows the camera already locks to. The building is walked through, front to back, the way the real one is laid out: lot and doors, the floor, a rear door, a covered patio, the rear lot. The back room is invented; no reference shows it.

The block, end to end

Window 0 · the floor · wave 0The crew starts at the doors under the pole sign. The stage is the far wall: dancers are scenery and run the tab. Pool tables are the hazard, the whole depth of the band.
Window 1 · the back room · wave 1Through the bead curtain. The sleeper is on the couch at the far end. Standing still over him for a second and a half puts him on a hen's back — the revive's rule and ring.
Window 2 · the rear door · wave 2 + bouncerThe carrier walks slow and cannot peck; a peck or a hit puts him down. One goon is always on him: it drags him back (red) until pecked. The camera never leaves him behind.
Exit run · patio and rear lotNo wave. The block is cleared when he crosses the line into the cab, on anybody's back. The bills still in his wallet are banked there.

The clock is his wallet

One clock on the wall. The marquee's clock slot shows the wallet instead of the block's cap: eight bills, one gone a song, the next one burning down. Empty is the bell.

Starting numbers for a prototype, not a tuning: 120 seconds, nothing charged for the first 50, then a bill every 10. A bill left when he reaches the cab is worth one for every hen in the crew, so the wallet is dealt by crew size like the rest of the kit.
