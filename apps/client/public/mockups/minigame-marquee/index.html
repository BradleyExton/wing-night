Minigame Marquee (TV) — Prototype Round

Six directions for the strip, plus two hybrids added after the first pick (07, 08), across the top of every minigame on the TV: the team, the show title, the counter and the clock. Each page cycles four real turns from the pack on click and the clock ticks live; press U to jump into the last fifteen seconds and see the urgent and time's-up states.

The question these answer: today all eight TV marquees wear the same brown gold-bordered box with a dotted bulb ring, and it reads as generic game-show chrome rather than as this show. These six disagree on purpose about what the strip is: a broadcast graphic, the team's banner, a bar sign, a bottle label, a scoreboard, or no chrome at all. All of them carry the same four facts in the same places, so any one can replace the current marquee behind the shared tokens without touching a game's body. The previews below are live at TV proportions; open one to see it full-screen. 07 was chosen on 2026-09-23 and built as <NeonMarquee> in packages/surface (DESIGN.md §2.2D, ADR-0006).

01

Broadcast Bug

A sports score bug: compact, left-anchored, skewed, hard-edged. LIVE tab, the team on a slab of its own colour, the title as a channel ident, the clock as a filled block that drains. Nothing spans the width, so the game's body gets the rest of the top row back.
02

Team Banner

The turn's team owns the header outright: its colour floods the bar and its genre face sets the name, so Molten Metal and Honky Tonk Heat get visibly different headers. The show title becomes a stamp. Leans on the team-identity system that already ships.

Costs the genre font and team colour reaching the display surface props, which today only the rail and lobby get.
03

Neon Sign

No box. The show title is a neon tube in Monoton with one tired letter that flickers, the team is Anton in white light, the clock is a glowing pill that turns red. Chrome made of light instead of material, which is what a bar with a wing night actually has on its wall.
04

Sauce Label

A hot-sauce bottle label: cream paper, a double rule, ticket-punched ends, the title in Rye with an EXTRA HOT ribbon, the counter as a chili heat rating and the clock in a red wax seal. The only light direction, and the one that says "wings" rather than "game show".

Breaks §0.1's palette: the paper and kraft browns are new material colours and would need a scoped exception.
05

Jumbotron

An arena LED board: black bezel with rivets, an amber dot matrix behind Orbitron, the clock as a red segment display. The bluntest of the six and the easiest to read across a room, at the cost of feeling like a hockey rink more than a kitchen.
06

Heat Line

Anti-chrome. A gold kicker, a huge team name in the house sans, and the clock as a full-width line that burns down from gold through orange to red, with a white-hot tip. Time is space, the digits are a footnote. The cheapest to build and the one with the least decoration to date.
07 · CHOSEN

Neon Heat Line · hybrid of 06 + 03

Heat Line's layout wearing Neon Sign's light: the kicker is a small Monoton tube with the flickering letter, the team name is Anton in white light, the digits sit in the glowing pill, and the burning line is itself a tube in a glass track with a white-hot tip.
08

Neon Sign, Burning · hybrid of 03 + 06

Neon Sign exactly as it was, except the gold rule under the sign is now the clock: a tube that burns down from the right edge. The centred tube title stays the hero; the digits lose their pill and become a footnote beside the counter.
