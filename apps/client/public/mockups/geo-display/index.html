Geo Display (TV) — Mockups

Three takes on the TV surface for GEO, all of them wearing the show's own clothes instead of the vintage-expedition costume the built surface wears today. Click anywhere to toggle the reveal.

The question these answer: GEO is the only minigame in the night dressed differently — sepia map tiles, serif type, a rotated polaroid, gold double-rules and a "Field Dossier" masthead. Every other game wears the bulb marquee and the house card (§2.5–2.11). These three put GEO in that same kit and then disagree about one thing: how much of the screen the photo deserves, and whether the map is a payoff or the stage. Shared across all three: the bulb marquee, dark map tiles (real OSM tiles under a CSS invert, replacing the sepia filter), reveal stats as house stat tiles rather than rotated rubber stamps, and sans typography. The photo is a CSS stand-in, not a real party photo. Deck footer is simplified for context.

01

Broadcast

The straight translation: today's layout, re-skinned. Marquee on top, photo in a house card on the left, title and hint on the right; the card flips to the dark map on reveal and the right column swaps its hint for Off-by / Points tiles. Least risk, smallest diff, and the photo ends up no bigger than it is now.

02

Full Bleed

The photo is the stage — edge to edge, no frame at all, with the marquee floating on top and the title, hint and status as a lower third. On reveal the photo pushes back and the dark map wipes up over the bottom two thirds. The most TV of the three, and the one that actually lets the room look at the photograph.

03

Map Theatre

Inverts it: the dark map owns the screen for the whole turn and the photo sits in a corner card, so the room watches the pin move while the team argues. The reveal is the answer pin landing and the line snapping between them. Costs a projection change — the TV currently never receives the team's in-progress guess, only the submitted result. The answer coordinates stay host-side either way.
