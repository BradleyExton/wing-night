## Pitch

Push-your-luck, dressed as the night itself. Twelve wings sit on a rack on the TV. The active
team's birds line up behind it in roster order. Tap: the first bird hops up and pecks a wing,
the wing flips, and it is worth something (a mild, a medium, a hot) or it is the Wing of Death,
and the bird's head goes up in flames and everything pecked this turn is gone. After every safe
peck the tablet asks one question, BANK or PECK AGAIN, and the whole team has to answer it out
loud before the holder taps. Nobody in the room can stay quiet through "one more".

It is the chance game the roster does not have (Caillois's alea, principles §6), and it is the
honest kind: the room chose to peck again, so the near miss is motivating rather than unfair
(research `C.3`, findings 1.4 and 4.5). It also costs almost nothing: no physics, no content,
no clock, three poses the cast already has (`peck`, `hurt`, `ko`).

## Rough rules

- Per-team turn. The rack is dealt from a seed per team (so every team faces the same odds, not
  the same rack): N wings, one Wing of Death, the rest valued in tiers. Values are hidden until
  pecked; the tier mix is a published rule on the briefing.
- Teammates peck in roster order, one wing each per pass; after the last teammate it wraps.
  Every peck is a tap on the tablet by whoever's bird is up, so the tablet goes round the table.
- After each safe peck: BANK ends the turn with the pile scored; PECK AGAIN continues. The Wing
  of Death wipes the pile, and the turn ends with the bird on fire and the room's reaction.
- The rack can be pecked clean. Clearing every safe wing is the full cap.
- Pending points apply at the phase boundary like every other minigame. Host can redo and
  override (`AGENTS.md` §11).

## Principles check

- 2 Commit before reveal: every peck is the commit; the wing flips on the TV after it.
- 6 A bad answer is still a plausible answer: there are no answers, only the stop/go call, and a
  team that banked at two wings is as funny as one that burned at eleven.
- 9 Whole team moves or speaks: each bird pecks under its own player's tap, and bank-or-peck is
  a team argument every time.
- 10 Information asymmetry, and when it collapses: nobody knows (the seed is server-side);
  collapses one wing at a time.
- 13 What the watching teams do: shout "one more" at a team they want to see burn, and keep count
  of how many safe wings are left, which the TV shows.
- 15 Rules in one verb line: "Peck a wing. Bank it, or peck again."
- 23 What the sauce does to it: the only pressure is a tap, so the sauce is in the mouth of the
  shouting, and a burning team is the one most likely to say "one more".

## Open questions

- Odds: how many wings and how many deaths. One death in twelve makes the full clear a real
  option; two makes banking early correct, which is less fun to watch.
- Does the Wing of Death stay dealt at a seeded slot, or does a rule move it (the Last Dab is
  always the last one left)? The former is honest and simple; the latter makes clearing the
  rack impossible, which kills the cap.
- Should the burnt bird take its team's *banked* pile too (classic Can't Stop) or only the turn's
  pile? Only the turn's: a wiped scoreboard is a verdict, not a punchline (§9).
